13.1 Summary

The single-modality model with expression only still holds the benchmark, with a mean external concordance (C) of 0.627 across five cohorts. That model does not use survival to learn its gene loadings. Since October 16 we audited the fitting algorithm and found four problems, listed below. They explain why attempts to let survival guide the loadings have failed, and why the joint expression and methylation model does not converge on real data. Five targeted fixes to the existing code each repaired a specific defect, but none produced a model that converges and recovers a low-variance prognostic program in simulation.

We are therefore rebuilding the method as an R package, ssbmf, from a written model specification. Every update in the package must improve one stated objective. Preliminary results from this morning show that the rebuilt model fixes monotonicity and convergence: every step improves the objective, all 180 simulation fits converge, and on the expression data it matches the benchmark (mean external C 0.628 against 0.627). It still does not find a low-variance prognostic program more often than a two-step method, and in the joint expression and methylation fit the methylation data dominate and no program is prognostic. Survival’s weak pull on the loadings is therefore a property of the model, from the relative size of the survival and reconstruction likelihoods, and not a coding error. Short tests on the current code continue alongside the rebuild. Two analyses that do not depend on a converged model are complete: a correction for an expression transform that was applied twice in three cohorts, which leaves the benchmark unchanged, and a comparison of survival-based and unsupervised gene screening in training cross-validation.

Decisions for this meeting: how to balance survival against reconstruction, which is now the central question, including whether a survival weight chosen by cross-validation is acceptable; the criteria that define a working model; the proposed preprocessing alignments, and whether to score the gene-screening comparison on the external cohorts.

13.2 Problems found in the current fitting algorithm

Survival has almost no influence on the loadings. The update for a loading F_jk combines a precision from reconstructing the expression data with a precision from the Cox model. In four saved fits, the survival share of that precision was between 6 × 10⁻¹¹ and 3 × 10⁻⁷ for every program with a nonzero coefficient. With a share that small the loadings are learned from reconstruction alone, whatever the survival data say. The model is then a two-step method in practice: the programs come from matrix factorization, and survival only chooses coefficients for them. A prognostic program that explains little expression variance cannot be found this way.

The joint expression and methylation fit does not converge, and pruning removes nothing on real data. The TCGA fit with seven starting programs stops at the 500-sweep limit with its risk scores still changing, and the same happens in all five training cross-validation folds. Pruning is meant to remove programs that the empirical-Bayes priors shrink to zero, but in these capped fits no program is removed. At 25,000 CpGs the Cox risk-set sums underflowed. Computing them on the log scale fixed that error, but the fit still hit the sweep limit, and its risk scores reached a standard deviation of about 39,000.

The fitting objective is not tracked consistently. Three steps change the model without the objective accounting for them. After the loading prior is refitted, the Kullback–Leibler terms are still computed against the previous prior, so the reported objective mixes two priors. The rescaling of programs inside the loop (L → cL, F → F/c, β → cβ) transforms the posterior moments but not the shared prior on β. The Cox term enters through a second-order expansion at the current linear predictor, and that expansion is not a lower bound on the Cox partial likelihood: refreshing it lowered the objective in 18 of 50 refreshes in one audit. Coordinate ascent on such a surrogate has no guarantee of improving anything, which is consistent with the oscillation and capped fits we see. The joint model’s Cox working weights also have no lower bound, unlike the single-modality code.

Survival coefficients can become stuck at zero. With a shared point-Laplace prior on β, the empirical-Bayes refit can set the prior’s nonzero mass to zero. Once it does, every coefficient’s posterior is a point mass at zero and no later update can move it. In the TCGA fit this happened within the first two inner iterations of sweep 99. With a separate Normal prior per program, the variance estimate max(0, x² − s²) is zero whenever the standardized coefficient falls below one, which produced an all-zero β in one cross-validation fold. That state is not permanent, though, and coefficients did return in earlier sweeps.

13.3 Fixes tried on the current code

Each fix was registered before it ran and judged by fixed criteria: convergence, recovery of a planted program in simulation, no loss in training cross-validated C, and coefficients credibly different from zero.

Fix What it repaired What remained
Log-scale Cox risk-set arithmetic The numerical underflow at 25,000 CpGs. The same likelihood is computed stably. The fit still stops at the sweep limit with extreme risk scores
Fixed (not refitted) shared prior on β Stops the coefficients from being absorbed at zero Single modality: 19 of 20 low-noise simulations converge, and CV C drops by 0.016 (SE 0.007). Joint model: no fit converges and no coefficient is credible
Profiled Cox intercept in the expansion Removes the constant direction from the Cox curvature Planted programs are recovered in 16 to 20 of 20 simulations, but all five joint CV fits and both full fits hit the limit
Loading prior refitted at a fixed posterior, with consistent Kullback–Leibler terms The prior and posterior no longer disagree Meets the objective’s stopping rule in 10 sweeps instead of 300, yet the loadings still change by 2% per sweep against a tolerance of 0.01%
Pruning that protects survival programs and keeps the fitted posterior when refitting Recovery of the planted program improves Fails the fixed rule on recovery, convergence and choice of rank. Rank selection by pruning is unresolved

Not yet tried on the current code: a lower bound of 1/K on the prior inclusion probability of β, a Normal β prior with a supervised start, longer warm-up, damping, a joint update of each program’s loadings and coefficient, and a survival weight. The rebuild takes these up in its derivation instead of adding more options to the existing fitters, which already carry about 30 option flags each.

13.4 Data preprocessing

Audit. We traced every preprocessing step for both analyses, from source file to model input, with the function that applies each. The two analyses differ in twelve places. The ones that matter most are a different TCGA expression source (RSEM values in the single-modality analysis, normalized counts per million in the joint analysis), per-cohort z-scores in one versus log-scale values with an optional intercept in the other, different scaling of validation cohorts, and different rules for features missing from a validation cohort. The full audit, with eleven proposed alignments, is in docs/findings/2026-10-26-preprocessing-audit.md.

Nonnegative factors on z-scored data. The single-modality model fits nonnegative L and F to z-scored expression, so 47% of the entries it reconstructs are negative. A product of nonnegative matrices cannot go below zero. In the benchmark fit, 69% of the reconstruction error falls on those negative entries, and almost all of it is the floor ΣY² that no nonnegative fit can go below. That floor alone is 55% of the total variation in the data. The fitted noise precisions absorb this structured error, and they set the weight of the reconstruction term against the survival term. The rebuilt model adds a feature intercept, and its provisional loading prior is signed (point-Laplace), so programs can describe values below the mean.

The double log transform. TCGA, Dijk and PACA-AU RNA-seq were transformed with log₂(x + 1) twice: once by the data loader and again by the project’s preprocessing. The second log compresses single-log values from 9 to 23 into 3.3 to 4.6. We refitted the benchmark with each cohort logged once and nothing else changed. A control refit of the original pipeline reproduced the frozen benchmark exactly.

13.5 Survival-associated gene screening

Question. Can a survival screen give the same prediction and programs with fewer genes than the current unsupervised mean and variance screen?

Programs. The benchmark fit has two survival-active programs, the adverse and protective programs described in earlier chapters. All three survival-screened fits recover both, with activity correlations of 0.87 to 0.97 and loading correlations of 0.93 to 0.99, and their risk scores correlate with the benchmark’s at 0.96 or more. What it means. The concordance differences are within about two standard errors, so they are suggestive at best. The program results are clearer. A 251-gene survival screen keeps the benchmark’s prognostic structure, and an unsupervised set of the same size does not. The screen supplies, through the choice of genes, the supervision that the current fitting algorithm fails to give the loadings. These results are conditional on scaling done before the folds and use one seed. External scoring of the frozen fits is the next step if you agree. If the rebuilt model lets survival guide the loadings, the comparison should be repeated with it.

13.6 Rebuilding the method as an R package

Why. The problems above come from how the algorithm was assembled, not from one bug. The fitters have grown by patches, each with its own option flags, and no single objective covers all their steps. A derivation-first rebuild is easier to check than every combination of those options.

Model. One model handles one or more data types for the same patients. Each block has its own feature intercept and loadings F_m, the patient activities L are shared across blocks, and survival enters through a Cox model on the projection scores, η = Σ_m Ỹ_m F_m β. New patients are scored in closed form from their molecular data. Expression alone is the one-block case of the same function, so there is one implementation. Priors are fitted by empirical Bayes. Cohorts enter as strata of the Cox model.

Fitting principles. Every coordinate update must not decrease one stated objective. The tests check this at every step instead of assuming it. The Cox term is replaced by a bound on its expected value, E log Σ_j exp(η_j) ≤ log Σ_j exp(η̄_j + v_j/2), where η̄_j and v_j are the posterior mean and variance of η_j. Unlike the current expansion, this keeps the objective a true lower bound. A fixed-curvature bound was also considered and rejected, because it overstates the survival precision by one to two orders of magnitude and would make the intervals for β too narrow. Each prior refit recomputes its Kullback–Leibler term, there is no rescaling inside the loop, and the β prior is chosen so that zero is not an absorbing state.

Interface. The package follows DeSurv’s layout: ssbmf_data() to validate inputs, ssbmf_fit() to fit, predict() for risk scores of new patients, coef() for coefficients with posterior intervals, ssbmf_classify() for program roles, and ssbmf_cv() for training cross-validation.

What counts as a working model. We propose these criteria and thresholds, to be fixed before results are seen:

Criterion Threshold
O1 Monotone objective No step lowers the objective by more than 10⁻⁸ of its magnitude
O2 Convergence Relative change below 10⁻⁶ within 1,000 sweeps in ≥ 95% of simulation fits and every real-data fit

Supervision is weak because of the model. In simulation, the survival share of the loading-update precision for the prognostic program had medians of 10⁻⁵ to 4 × 10⁻⁴. On the real expression data it was about 5 × 10⁻⁷, the same order as in the current code. The objective is now computed correctly, so the coding errors were not the cause. The model counts every matrix entry as an independent Gaussian observation. That gives reconstruction 10³ to 10⁶ times the information the Cox model contributes to a program’s loadings, so the loadings follow the reconstruction.

Balance options tried. Two options were tried in the planned order on the low-variance settings. A weight on each block by its effective number of features lowered the reconstruction weight to 0.06–0.23. Recovery then fell, to 0 of 15 in the hardest setting, so the option was dropped. A survival weight chosen by training cross-validation from {1, 10, 100} made the 0.25% program survival-active in 12 of 15 datasets and raised validation C from 0.543 to 0.603, above the two-step method’s 0.584. The recovered loadings were a mixture, with a mean correlation with the planted program of 0.40 against the 0.7 threshold. The weighted likelihood also made the β intervals far too narrow: 90% intervals covered the truth in only 27% of fits. When the program is easy to find, cross-validation keeps a weight of 1 or 10 and nothing changes.

Expression-only real data. The setup matches the benchmark: the same 273 patients and 2,064 genes, cohorts as Cox strata. Training cross-validation over 5, 7, 10 and 15 starting programs gave mean C of 0.640 to 0.656. The one-standard-error rule chose 5. The frozen fit converged in 297 sweeps, with two survival-active programs and three reconstruction-only programs. Their coefficients per SD of the program score are 0.47 (95% interval 0.32 to 0.62) and −0.19 (−0.35 to −0.04).

Expression and methylation, TCGA to ICGC. Training used 144 TCGA patients with 3,000 genes and 10,000 CpGs. Both the joint and the expression-only fit converged. In the joint fit, the empirical-Bayes prior on β shrank to a variance of about 2 × 10⁻⁷, so all three programs are reconstruction-only and the risk score carries no information. Its first program explains 35% of the methylation variance and 8% of the expression variance. The expression-only fit has two survival-active programs and an ICGC C of 0.707 on 50 donors. The same counting of matrix entries lets 10,000 CpGs outweigh 3,000 genes, now with a correct objective.

What this means. The rebuild fixes the algorithm: the objective is a true bound, every step improves it, fits converge with one or two data types, and expression-only prediction matches the benchmark. It does not fix supervision, and the joint model is dominated by methylation. Both come from how the likelihood weighs survival against reconstruction and one data type against the other. How to set that balance is now the central question. The options still open are a survival weight chosen by cross-validation (at the cost of calibrated β intervals), supervision on a smaller survival-screened feature set (the screening result above points this way), per-block weights for the joint model chosen by training cross-validation, or a methylation set reduced to the size of the expression set.

13.7 Work continuing on the current method

Two short tests on the existing code answer questions the rebuild also needs. The first asks whether survival can guide the loadings in the single-modality model once its contribution is made larger: fits on the planted-program simulation with survival weights of 1, 10², 10⁴ and 10⁶, measuring the survival share of the loading precision, recovery of the planted program, convergence, held-out C and reconstruction error. The second adds four controls to the joint model, one per bookkeeping problem above, and asks whether it then converges on TCGA. Each test stops after two failed attempts, and its finding passes to the rebuild.

Several experiments wait for a converged joint model: the CpG-count and joint expression-by-CpG curves, program roles across starting ranks, and the CpG version of the screening comparison.

13.8 Questions for the advisors

  1. The rebuilt model converges, but survival still barely shapes the loadings, and methylation outweighs expression in the joint fit, because every matrix entry counts as one observation. How should the terms be balanced? A survival weight chosen by training cross-validation finds prognostic signal but breaks the calibration of the β intervals. Is that acceptable, or should supervision act on a smaller survival-screened feature set, or should the model be changed so that it sets the balance itself? For the joint model, should block weights be chosen by training cross-validation, or should the methylation set be reduced to the size of the expression set?
  2. Are criteria O1–O8 and their thresholds the right definition of a working model? Should any be relaxed or added before more results come in? In particular, should O4 be judged at 0.25% or at 1% of variance explained?
  3. Which of the proposed preprocessing alignments should we adopt? In particular: one log for every RNA-seq cohort, an intercept with log-scale data in place of z-scores, and one TCGA expression source for both analyses.
  4. Should the frozen gene-screening fits be scored once on the five external cohorts now, or after the rebuilt model is available?
