2  May 7, 2026

Several fixes landed this week that changed how much we trust the merged-cohort results, plus the first head-to-head between the two model formulations, LB and YFB.

2.1 Method updates

  • Risk-direction sign fix (5/5). Both LB and YFB had an anti-concordance bug: the model’s risk score was pointing the wrong way for a subset of fits. Fixed; every external result from this point on assumes the corrected sign.
  • Merged YFB collapse resolved (5/6). The 5/5 report still showed merged YFB collapsing to \(\beta\approx0\). Switching to per-platform z-standardization and dropping the rank transform recovered non-zero, non-degenerate survival coefficients on merged TCGA+CPTAC. This supersedes the 5/5 result.
  • Ruled out as fixes: constrained SVD initialization + Cox-sign alignment (no ELBO or external gain), and a 30-restart multi-start sweep (plain SVD initialization already wins on ELBO, so restarts aren’t the bottleneck).
  • K-selection pipeline. Implemented K-fold CV for choosing the number of factors. Reliable for LB; not yet reliable for YFB (see Open items).

LB vs. YFB, as a modeling choice: LB puts prognosis on the latent loadings (\(\eta=L\beta\)) and needs an OLS approximation to score new subjects. YFB puts prognosis on the observed factor scores (\(\eta=(YF)\beta\)) and scores new subjects exactly, with no approximation. The tradeoff is that YFB needed its own from-scratch derivation rather than reusing LB’s update rules.

LB is the stable benchmark for now. It gives essentially the same predictions under both the normal and point_normal priors, and its best single-cohort result (PACA-AU seq) reaches 0.656–0.673. YFB is mixed outside the merged, per-platform-standardized setting: single-cohort YFB fits give near-zero betas even when the raw C-index looks above chance, which isn’t a convincing signal on its own. On the merged cohort with the right preprocessing, though, YFB matches LB’s 0.644. That is the first evidence YFB can match LB’s performance once the platform-scale issue is handled.

K-selection: under the current CV sweep, LB’s normal prior picks \(K=3\) (1-SE rule); point_normal picks \(K=8\), read as a shrinkage artifact from a small \(n\) rather than real evidence for 8 factors. Practical compromise for LB: \(K=5\). YFB’s CV isn’t decision-ready yet: point-normal collapses to chance (C=0.5) in every fold, and the normal prior gives anti-concordant results across folds because held-out folds don’t yet have stable sign correction.

2.3 Relevance of new findings

The sign fix matters retroactively; any external number reported before 5/5 needs to be read as provisional. The bigger result is that merged YFB, once given per-platform z-standardization, reaches parity with LB (0.644 both) instead of collapsing. This is the first real evidence that the YFB reformulation from the previous entry is viable, not just a cleaner derivation on paper.

2.4 Open items to address

  • Should K-fold CV stay the operational rule for LB while YFB’s K-selection issue is still open?
  • Next methodological push: Bayesian CV, or ARD-style shrinkage from a large \(K_\text{max}\)?
  • Single-cohort YFB still needs a scale-aware fix analogous to what worked for the merged cohort.
  • LB’s loading update still looks genomics-dominated; survival’s influence on \(L\) may still be too weak even after the sign fix.
