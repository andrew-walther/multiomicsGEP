SSBMF Status Update: Preprocessing Comparison, Cohort Indicator, and Final Configuration (May 27, 2026)

Author

Andrew Walther

1. Objectives and Decisions Addressed Since Last Update

This report covers three questions that arose from the May 8 update and have now been resolved through a comprehensive 18-configuration benchmark on merged TCGA_PAAD + CPTAC training data (\(n = 273\), 5-fold CV K-selection with biological floor \(K \geq 3\), evaluated on 5 external cohorts).

  • Which preprocessing strategy is compatible with merged multi-platform training? Five strategies compared: joint quantile normalization + rank transform (prior standard), per-platform z-standardization, joint QN without rank transform, joint z-standardization, and log-transform only.

  • Does the cohort indicator column in \(L\) improve generalization? The cohort indicator absorbs the RNA-seq vs. proteomics platform offset at the model level (as opposed to removing it in preprocessing). Evaluated for both LB and YFB across preprocessing strategies.

  • LB vs. YFB for the final manuscript configuration? LB (\(\eta = L\beta\)) requires an OLS approximation to score new subjects (\(\hat{L}_{\text{new}} \approx Y_{\text{new}} F (F^\top F)^{-1}\)). YFB (\(\eta = (YF)\beta\)) scores new subjects exactly (\(\eta_{\text{new}} = Y_{\text{new}} F \beta\)) with no approximation.

Critical finding: 10 of 12 non-per-platform configurations produce \(\hat{\beta} = 0\) for all factors (\(K_{\text{eff}} = 0\)). The only viable preprocessing for merged RNA-seq + proteomics training is per-platform z-standardization applied before merging.

3. Preprocessing: Why Per-Platform Z-Standardization Is Required

When TCGA_PAAD (RNA-seq) and CPTAC (proteomics) expression matrices are stacked without per-platform normalization, the top singular vectors of the merged \(Y\) matrix capture the platform contrast, not biological variation. The CAVI survival update receives near-zero signal in these directions, so \(\hat{\beta} \to 0\) regardless of model form or further normalization. Rank transform (M13/M14 vs. joint QN): removing the rank transform from joint-QN data does prevent YFB collapse, ruling out rank transform as the mechanism. The root cause is the platform-dominated SVD shared by all joint preprocessing strategies.

Per-platform z-standardization removes each cohort’s gene-level mean and variance merging. This makes the joint expression matrix reflect within-platform biological variation rather than between-platform scale differences. After per-platform z-std, both LB and YFB recover non-zero \(\hat{\beta}\) at \(K = 3\).

4. Cohort Indicator: When It Helps vs. When It Hurts

The cohort indicator appends a platform dummy to \(L\) and estimates a cohort-specific gene profile \(F_{\text{coh}}\) via Normal conjugate update. Its effect depends on whether preprocessing has already removed the platform offset:

Setting Cohort indicator effect Mechanism
LB + joint QN (M1→M2) +0.020 Absorbs platform offset; frees 2 extra biological factors
LB + per-platform z-std (M3→M4) +0.004 Marginal; preprocessing already removed bulk offset
YFB + per-platform z-std (M5→M6) −0.013 At \(K=3\), cohort column competes for variance with 2 biological factors
LB + joint QN, low \(K\) (K=5) Essential Without it, LB also collapses to \(K_{\text{eff}}=0\) at \(K=5\)

Recommendation on cohort indicator: It is most valuable as a robustness feature for LB when per-platform preprocessing cannot be guaranteed (e.g., adding a new cohort to an existing fit without retraining). For the primary analysis configuration (YFB + per-platform z-std), it is at low \(K\) and should be excluded. For sensitivity analysis (LB + per-platform z-std, M4), it is effectively neutral (+0.004) and can be included without cost. Future work extending to 3+ cohorts should revisit the cohort indicator, as it may provide greater benefit when platform contrast is larger.

5. Recommendation: Final Configuration for Manuscript

Recommended configurations. D4 (primary) uses K=7 (CV-selected) with DeSurv-aligned gene selection; M4 (sensitivity) uses K=3 (biological floor).
Role Model Preprocessing Cohort K K_eff Mean C Notes
Primary (D4) YFB Per-platform z-std + DeSurv gene selection No 7 2 0.636 DeSurv-aligned gene selection (+0.012 vs M5); 2 active programs; highest C
M4 Sensitivity (M4) LB Per-platform z-std Yes 3 1 0.624 Explicit platform offset model; LB robustness check

D4 (primary; supersedes M5): YFB with DeSurv-aligned gene selection (combined mean+variance rank, top-3000 genes per cohort selected on log-transformed data before per-platform z-standardization) identifies 2 active gene expression programs (\(K_{\text{eff}} = 2\)) from 7 learned factors, with mean external C = 0.636 across 5 independent PDAC cohorts (Dijk, Moffitt GEO, PACA-AU array, PACA-AU seq, Puleo). This supersedes M5 (mean C = 0.626, K=3): the DeSurv gene selection yields \(\Delta C = +0.012\), exceeding the pre-specified \(\pm 0.005\) practical threshold, with improvement in 4 of 5 external cohorts. New-patient risk scores require only \(\eta_{\text{new}} = Y_{\text{new}} F \hat\beta\) — no sample-level latent variable approximation. Factor loadings \(F\) are directly interpretable as gene weights defining each prognostic program. See docs/reports/desurv_alignment_report_05_27_26.pdf for the full D1–D4 comparison.

M4 (sensitivity): LB with cohort indicator reaches mean C = 0.624 — effectively tied with M5 — and explicitly encodes the RNA-seq vs. proteomics platform difference as a model parameter rather than a preprocessing step.

7. Open Items and Next Steps

  • Pathway enrichment on D4 factors. DeSurv-aligned gene selection changes the training gene set (2064 genes vs 2000); re-run GSEA/ORA on D4 factor loadings once adopted.

  • Gene set enrichment / pathway analysis for D4 (formerly M5) factors 1 and 2 (the 2 active prognostic programs). This is the primary interpretability task remaining before the manuscript methods section can be finalized.

  • Cohort indicator for future multi-cohort extensions. As the training set grows beyond 2 platforms (e.g., adding microarray cohorts), the cohort indicator may become more valuable. The cohort_id parameter is already implemented in both LB and YFB; the cost/benefit analysis above applies per-pair of preprocessing choices and \(K\).

  • Single-cohort YFB. YFB on TCGA-only or CPTAC-only still has near-zero betas (lack of platform contrast means the SVD directions are more biological, but \(n\) is too small for stable CV). Not blocking for the merged-cohort manuscript.

  • LB prediction approximation. The OLS approximation for LB new-subject scoring is adequate for sensitivity analysis but should be noted as a limitation vs. YFB. A potential alternative: train YFB on TCGA-only + CPTAC-only separately and compare factor consistency across platforms as a validation experiment.
