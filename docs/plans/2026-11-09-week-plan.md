Five tasks on the single-modality model and its extension to several data types (A to
E), a redo on training data of earlier choices that used the external cohorts (F), and a
design discussion for keeping results current (G). Every design and success criterion below is written before any fit is run. Nothing here

The working model is `fit_ssbmf_yfb(canonicalize = TRUE, w_S = 1)` on the 2,218 D4 genes,

## Why the priors are reopened

The project set out to fit a nonnegative factorization: point-exponential L and F on
nonnegative expression, as in DeSurv, so that each program is an additive set of genes and
each patient a nonnegative mix of programs. Point-Laplace replaced it on 2026-10-15 for a
narrow reason. On z-standardized input the scores are signed, and the point-exponential
prior collapsed to zero programs in 6 of 10 test-bed seeds. That decision entry says the
real-data refit "will have to settle the L/F prior". It was never settled.

The obstacle is now gone. `center_projection = TRUE` (DECISIONS.md, 2026-11-06) keeps Y
nonnegative for the reconstruction and centers it only inside the Cox projection, which
removes the term that shrank β to zero on uncentered input. A like-for-like comparison of
the two priors is therefore possible, and the remaining tasks inherit whatever prior it
chooses.

## Data: external cohorts are for validation scoring only

Decided by Andrew on 2026-11-09 (DECISIONS.md, same date). Every modeling choice, from
preprocessing and gene screening to priors, rank, survival weight and comparator tuning, is
made by simulation and by five-fold cross-validation on the 273 TCGA and CPTAC training
patients (the matched TCGA patients for methylation). The five external expression series
(Dijk, Moffitt, PACA-AU array, PACA-AU RNA-seq, Puleo) and the matched ICGC methylation set
are used only to score a model that is already fixed. Their results can be presented, but
they never choose between models or settings. Earlier choices that did use external
outcomes ([external-cohort audit](../findings/2026-11-06-external-cohort-uses.md)) are
redone on training data in task F. No external cohort is scored this week.

**How DeSurv used the same seven cohorts.** DeSurv (Young et al., PNAS 2026, Methods
section C and SI Appendix sections 5, 6, 8 and 14) trained on TCGA and CPTAC pooled and
chose every hyperparameter (k, α, λ, ξ, n_top) by maximizing five-fold cross-validated
concordance inside the training data, with the one-SE rule for k. The paper states that no
validation cohort data were used at any stage of tuning. It held out no separate portion
of the training data. Its folds are described only as "divide subjects into F folds", so
the paper does not say whether they were balanced by cohort. The five external cohorts
were used once, to score the final model by projection, Z = W′X_new, standardized with the
training mean and SD. Pooled external Cox models and log-rank tests were stratified by
dataset. Two external analyses were run over a grid after selection and reported as
sensitivity, not used for choice: the k × α table of adjusted external P-values (Tables
S2 to S4) and the external C-index curve over k (Fig. S3). The risk-group cutpoint was
chosen by training CV and then applied unchanged to the external cohorts. Our S-11 to
S-15 work follows the same pattern, so this week's training-only rule matches DeSurv's
protocol.

**No held-out portion of the training data (decided by Andrew, 2026-11-09).** A fixed held-out
portion of the 273 patients would act as a sealed test set for the final choice. Its size
is the problem. In S-11 the five-fold CV concordance had SE 0.024 across folds, which
implies a fold-to-fold SD of about 0.05 for a fold of about 55 patients. A 20% hold-out
would therefore estimate C with an SE near 0.05, while the differences between the arms
compared this week have been 0.002 to 0.01. It could not separate them, and it would remove
a fifth of the patients from every fit. So, as in DeSurv, there is no held-out portion.
Choices use five-fold CV on all 273 patients, with folds balanced by cohort and event so
that every training fold has both cohorts (needed for the cohort strata). If
split-to-split noise matters for a close paired comparison, the five-fold split is
repeated with new seeds rather than patients being set aside. Where a step has its own
tuning inside the fit, such as a CV-chosen weight, the outer estimate uses nested CV, as
in S-15.

## Task A. Priors on L and F

**Question.** On nonnegative input with the Cox projection centered, does the
point-exponential prior on L and F recover a planted prognostic program, with controlled
false activity and convergence, at least as well as the current z-scored point-Laplace
fit? On the training data, does it predict as well under cross-validation?

**Arms.** Both use `canonicalize = TRUE`, `w_S = 1`, Normal β, K = 7, no pruning, at most
300 sweeps and tolerance 10⁻⁵.

1. PL (current): z-score within cohort, `prior_LF = "point_laplace"`.
2. PE: X/sd within cohort (nonnegative), `prior_LF = "point_exponential"`,
   `center_projection = TRUE`.

**Simulation.** The S-9 generator, `yfb_decision_data(seed, p, v, truth_mode = "shared")`
in `results/benchmark_sim/run_yfb_supervision_decision.R`: seeds 1 to 10, p = 2,000,
planted variance shares 0.25%, 1% and 5%, n = 400 training and 120 validation patients,
30 datasets. Its planted L and F are drawn from exponential distributions, so they are
nonnegative and neither prior is favored by the truth. The generator returns X/sd, which
is the PE input, and PL takes its within-cohort z-score, as in S-9. Add the S-14
near-threshold cell (planted share 0.05%, strong survival) from
`results/benchmark_sim/run_yfb_near_threshold.R`, 10 seeds, because that is where
supervision mattered. If saved S-9 and S-14 PL fits reproduce under the exact-time
survival fix (`timefix = FALSE`), reuse them. Otherwise refit PL.

**Real data.** The 273 training patients and the five-fold split used in S-11 and S-15.
Report fold-wise and mean CV concordance with `frozen_reverse_cindex()` and the paired
PE minus PL difference with its SE.

*Cohort strata (decided by Andrew, 2026-11-09).* X/sd keeps each cohort's mean level, and
the fit is pooled over TCGA and CPTAC. A single projection center would leave the
TCGA-CPTAC mean difference in the Cox projection. Both arms therefore use
`strata_id = cohort`, which centers the projection within cohort and gives each cohort its
own baseline hazard, acknowledging that the two cohorts differ. Stratification was
performance-neutral for D4 (DECISIONS.md, 2026-07-15), and DeSurv stratified its pooled
external analyses by dataset in the same way. PL gets the same strata as PE, so only the
prior and the input differ. Because the PL baseline changes from the unstratified S-11
fit, also run the unstratified PL fit once on the same folds as the link to S-11 and S-15.
Record this choice in DECISIONS.md when task A starts.

*Scale check under strata.* Score standardization under `strata_id` uses the
within-stratum SD. A program that mainly separates TCGA from CPTAC has a small
within-stratum SD, gets rescaled by a large factor, and can then fall under the fitter's
absolute dead-program floors (score SD below 10⁻⁶, max(A_gen + w_S A_surv) below 10⁻⁸)
even though it explains real expression variation. The handoff's scaling diagnostic gives
a reproducible example built from a fixed constructed state, not a fit. For every real-data fit in A, record
each program's within-stratum and global score SD, its canonical scale divisor, and its
largest A_gen. If any program is zeroed by a floor while its divisor exceeds 100 or its
global SD is above 0.1, the stratified fit is not interpreted. A scale-robust way to tell
a real genomics-only program from a collapsed one is then needed first. That change would
get its own evaluation, planned before running, of planted recovery, false-active
programs and convergence, and is not part of this week.

*What the option has and has not shown.* On the October 15 planted bed, centering the
projection let survival reach β (without it, no program was survival-active in 6 of 6
seeds). Loading recovery stayed weak (best correlation 0.08 to 0.84), and in 4 of 6 seeds
the active program was not the planted one. The option is a prerequisite for a fair
comparison, not evidence that supervision works on nonnegative input. That is why A is
judged on the Gate S quantities and not on whether β moves.

**What is compared.** Per fit: recovery (best |r| between the planted direction and E[F_k]
at least 0.7, the matched program survival-active with correct sign), number of false-active
programs, convergence flag, validation C and calibration slope, as defined in the S-9 plan.
On real data, also the number of live programs, whether one program absorbs the mean
expression level (correlation of its scores with each patient's mean expression), and gene
overlap of the top 100 genes of each PE program with programs 2 and 3 of the current fit.

**Success criteria (PE is adopted if all hold).**

1. Recovery in every simulation cell is at least PL's minus 1 of 10.
2. Mean false-active programs per fit is at most 1 in every cell.
3. At least 9 of 10 fits converge in every cell.
4. Real-data CV concordance is no lower than PL's by more than one SE of the paired
   difference, and every fold converges.

PE is the default this test is checking, because it is the intended model. If it fails
any criterion, PL stays and the failure is recorded with the cell that failed. If PE
passes but uses a program for the mean level, K is not raised in this task. That is
reported, and the rank question goes to the rank-selection work.

**Files.** New runner `results/benchmark_sim/run_lf_prior_comparison.R` (simulation and
training CV in one script, output under `results/benchmark_sim/outputs/lf_prior_2026-11-09/`),
attempt entry in `docs/plans/2026-10-16-round2-attempts-log.md`, findings note, DECISIONS.md,
PROJECT_STATUS.qmd. No change to fitter defaults.

point-Laplace, point-normal or Normal?

## Task E. LB vs YFB with fold-in prediction

**Question.** With the same input and priors, how do the two survival parameterizations
differ in prediction and in what their programs mean? LB models the hazard through the
latent scores, η = Lβ. YFB models it through the projection of the data on the loadings,
η = (YF)β.

**Interpretation to aim for.** DeSurv factors X ≈ WH with nonnegative gene weights W, and
scores a patient by Z = W′X, a weighted sum of gene levels. In YFB, F plays the role of W
and YF is exactly that projection score. In LB, L is the factor-model score itself: the
posterior of each patient's program activity given all programs at once. With nonnegative
F the two are related by YF ≈ L(F′F) plus noise, so YF mixes the latent scores through the
overlap between programs, and the two coincide only when programs share no genes. For new
patients, YFB needs only Y_new F. LB needs fold-in, which solves for L_new under the stored
priors (`predict_ssbmf_lb_foldin()`). The study should say which score each figure and
coefficient refers to, and what a hazard ratio per unit of each means.

**Design.** Supersedes the ROADMAP's planned LB vs YFB study, with its alignment
requirements kept: the same preprocessed Y (X/sd if A chooses PE), the same priors on L,
F and β from A and B, the same initialization and K = 7. The LB arm separates the survival
weight from the (1 − α) tempering, and the LB dead-program start is controlled by the
same starting values as YFB.

- Simulation: the A cells, and the 2026-11-06 matched comparison settings
  (`results/benchmark_sim/run_lb_yfb_matched_comparison.R`) at w = 1 and 10.
- Training data: five-fold CV on the 273 patients, with cohort strata as in task A. LB's
  held-out fold is scored by fold-in and YFB's by projection, which is the same contrast
  as on an external cohort.
- External scoring waits until the hold-out question above is settled.

**What is compared.** Recovery, false activity, convergence and validation C in
simulation. Training CV concordance and its paired difference. Program content from one
fit per model on all 273 patients: top genes, overlap with each other and with DeSurv's
programs. The two linear predictors on the same patients: their correlation and the
per-program correlations between L_k and (YF)_k.

**Success criteria.** This task describes the two models and has no adoption rule unless
one model fails A's recovery, false-activity or convergence criteria in a cell where the
other passes. In that case, the failing model is not carried forward.

**Files.** Extend `results/benchmark_sim/run_lb_yfb_matched_comparison.R` or a new
`run_lb_yfb_study.R`, findings note, and a short interpretation section suitable for the
progress book.

## Task G. A results list that rebuilds when the model changes (design only)

**Goal.** Never present a result from an outdated model, and always know which model state
produced each result. This task writes a design for discussion and builds nothing.

**Starting points.** The ROADMAP already has an open item to investigate the R `targets`
package. `targets` records each step (data, fit, table, figure, report) and its inputs,
including the code of the functions it calls. When a function such as `fit_ssbmf_yfb()`
changes, every downstream result is marked out of date and `tar_make()` reruns only those.
A Makefile does the same from file timestamps. A Claude Code hook could remind a session to
rebuild after editing fitter code, but it cannot do the rebuild tracking itself.

**What the design should settle.**

1. An inventory of the results to keep current: simulation recovery tables, training CV,
   external validation scores, sensitivity analyses, program biology and figures in the
   progress book, the writeup and the prelim. Each entry gets its script, inputs, outputs
   and the documents that cite it.
2. Which of those are "core" and rerun on every relevant model change, and which are
   historical records that stay frozen with their commit (for example the October 14
   benchmark snapshot).
3. How each output records its model state: commit hash and fitter options written next to
   every saved table.
4. Whether `targets` fits the repository: long fits, patient data outside Git, Longleaf runs
   and ignored `data/` checkpoints. A smaller start is a numbered list of runner scripts plus
   a check that flags any output older than the fitter code it depends on.
5. Where the inventory lives so that it is not a second copy of PROJECT_STATUS.qmd.

**Output.** A design note in `docs/plans/` for Andrew to review. It should also say how it
fits the package-and-paper plan, since SSBMF-paper is meant to regenerate results from the
package.

## Pruning, set aside until the rank is chosen by CV (Andrew to confirm)

After the KL fixes, KL-1 and KL-2 gave the October answer again on the October 15 bed at
starting K = 7. Unpruned YFB recovered the planted program in 17 and 19 of 20 seeds.
Ordinary and protected pruning recovered it in 2 of 20 and collapsed to one or two
programs. The working fit stays at K = 7 without pruning.

The remedy aimed at the actual failure is `prune_preserve_posterior = TRUE`: the planted
program is lost when the fit restarts after a deletion, and this option keeps the full
posterior instead of restarting. On the pre-fix code it recovered 15 to 19 of 20 but failed
convergence and rank. It has not been run since either fix. A rerun is about 80 fits and
under an hour locally, with its design and criteria written before running. Starting ranks
5, 10 and 15 matter only if that arm passes.

Pruning was meant to start from a large K_init and remove programs that contribute to
neither reconstruction nor survival. That is one way to choose the rank. Task F chooses the
rank the way DeSurv did, by five-fold training CV with the one-SE rule, which needs no
pruning and avoids the restart failure. The recommendation is to set pruning aside until
task F's rank selection is done, and to return to the full-posterior rerun only if the
CV-chosen rank turns out unstable across folds or seeds. If that happens, run it under
A's chosen prior, since the prior changes what pruning sees.
