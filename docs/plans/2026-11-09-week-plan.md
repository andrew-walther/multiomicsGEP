## Why the priors are reopened

The project set out to fit a nonnegative factorization: point-exponential L and F on
nonnegative expression, as in DeSurv, so that each program is an additive set of genes and
each patient a nonnegative mix of programs. Point-Laplace replaced it on 2026-10-15 for a
narrow reason. On z-standardized input the scores are signed, and the point-exponential
prior collapsed to zero programs in 6 of 10 test-bed seeds. That decision entry says the
real-data refit "will have to settle the L/F prior". It was never settled.

The obstacle is now gone. `center_projection = TRUE` (DECISIONS.md, 2026-11-06) keeps Y
nonnegative for the reconstruction and centers it only inside the Cox projection, which
removes the term that shrank β to zero on uncentered input. A like-for-like comparison of
the two priors is therefore possible, and the remaining tasks inherit whatever prior it
chooses.

## Data: external cohorts are for validation scoring only

Decided by Andrew on 2026-11-09 (DECISIONS.md, same date). Every modeling choice, from
preprocessing and gene screening to priors, rank, survival weight and comparator tuning, is
made by simulation and by five-fold cross-validation on the 273 TCGA and CPTAC training
patients (the matched TCGA patients for methylation). The five external expression series
(Dijk, Moffitt, PACA-AU array, PACA-AU RNA-seq, Puleo) and the matched ICGC methylation set
are used only to score a model that is already fixed. Their results can be presented, but
they never choose between models or settings. Earlier choices that did use external
outcomes ([external-cohort audit](../findings/2026-11-06-external-cohort-uses.md)) are
redone on training data in task F. External scoring can be run at any time for reporting,
since it never feeds back into a choice. This week it is run once on Thursday, after the
methods work, on the working model as fixed by A, B and F, so that the writeup and
Chapter 4 report simulation, training CV and external results for the same model.

**How DeSurv used the same seven cohorts.** DeSurv (Young et al., PNAS 2026, Methods
section C and SI Appendix sections 5, 6, 8 and 14) trained on TCGA and CPTAC pooled and
chose every hyperparameter (k, α, λ, ξ, n_top) by maximizing five-fold cross-validated
concordance inside the training data, with the one-SE rule for k. The paper states that no
validation cohort data were used at any stage of tuning. It held out no separate portion
of the training data. Its folds are described only as "divide subjects into F folds", so
the paper does not say whether they were balanced by cohort. The five external cohorts
were used once, to score the final model by projection, Z = W′X_new, standardized with the
training mean and SD. Pooled external Cox models and log-rank tests were stratified by
dataset. Two external analyses were run over a grid after selection and reported as
sensitivity, not used for choice: the k × α table of adjusted external P-values (Tables
S2 to S4) and the external C-index curve over k (Fig. S3). The risk-group cutpoint was
chosen by training CV and then applied unchanged to the external cohorts. Our S-11 to
S-15 work follows the same pattern, so this week's training-only rule matches DeSurv's
protocol.

**No held-out portion of the training data (decided by Andrew, 2026-11-09).** A fixed held-out
portion of the 273 patients would act as a sealed test set for the final choice. Its size
is the problem. In S-11 the five-fold CV concordance had SE 0.024 across folds, which
implies a fold-to-fold SD of about 0.05 for a fold of about 55 patients. A 20% hold-out
would therefore estimate C with an SE near 0.05, while the differences between the arms
compared this week have been 0.002 to 0.01. It could not separate them, and it would remove
a fifth of the patients from every fit. So, as in DeSurv, there is no held-out portion.
Choices use five-fold CV on all 273 patients, with folds balanced by cohort and event so
that every training fold has both cohorts (needed for the cohort strata). The S-11 runner
already builds folds this way, but `create_stratified_folds()`, which `select_K_cv()` and
`cv_survival_loglik()` call, balances by event only. Before task A, give those helpers a
cohort-and-event fold option or a way to pass precomputed folds, with a test that every
training fold contains both cohorts. A paired comparison counts as close when the
difference is within one SE. Then the five-fold split is repeated with four more seeds
rather than patients being set aside. Nested CV is needed only where a step tunes itself
inside the fit, as the survival weight did in S-15. No task this week does that, since
w_S stays at 1.

## Reporting rules for all tasks

  without it, because the second-order Cox surrogates differ. Never choose a prior, K or

latent scores, η = Lβ. YFB models it through the projection of the data on the loadings,

**Interpretation to aim for.** DeSurv factors X ≈ WH with nonnegative gene weights W, and
scores a patient by Z = W′X, a weighted sum of gene levels. In YFB, F plays the role of W
and YF is exactly that projection score. In LB, L is the factor-model score itself: the
posterior of each patient's program activity given all programs at once. With nonnegative
F the two are related by YF ≈ L(F′F) plus noise, so YF mixes the latent scores through the
overlap between programs, and the two coincide only when programs share no genes. For new
patients, YFB needs only Y_new F. LB needs fold-in, which solves for L_new under the stored
priors (`predict_ssbmf_lb_foldin()`). The study should say which score each figure and
coefficient refers to, and what a hazard ratio per unit of each means.

2. **Input scaling.** Per-cohort standardization without a rank transform (M5) was chosen
   on external C (May 25). Task A compares z-scores with X/sd. Add DeSurv's within-subject
   rank transform as a third input under the chosen prior, by the same training CV.

   *Added by Andrew, 2026-11-11: rank transform with point-exponential priors.* Whatever
   prior task A chooses, also fit one fixed comparison arm that keeps the factorization
   nonnegative, following DeSurv's approach: rank genes within each patient
   (`rank_transform_subjects()`, so every entry is positive), then divide each gene by its
   within-cohort SD of the ranks without centering (the X/sd step of A's PE arm), with
   `prior_LF = "point_exponential"`, `center_projection = TRUE` and `strata_id = cohort`,
   as in A's PE arm. Call it "rank transform, per-gene SD scaling" in the findings. It is
   guided by DeSurv's preprocessing, not a copy of it: DeSurv feeds raw ranks to its NMF.

   *Rank scale (recommended and adopted for this arm, 2026-11-11).* Divide by the per-gene
   SD, not raw ranks (1 to p) or ranks divided by p. Raw ranks and ranks divided by p
   give the same input after this step, because the 1/p factor cancels. Reasons:
   (i) The likelihood is close to scale-free, since per-gene noise precisions and
   empirical-Bayes prior scales absorb a global rescaling, but the fitter's fixed
   thresholds are not. The dead-program floor (information below 10⁻⁸,
   `fit_ssbmf_yfb.R:1163`) is noise precision times summed squared scores (`:1154`), which
   after canonical rescaling grows with the square of the data scale. The collapsed-projection
   floor (SD below 10⁻⁶, `:185`) and the initial variance floor (10⁻⁸, `:958`) are also fixed.
   Raw ranks give per-gene SDs in the hundreds, which switches the floors off in effect and
   runs the empirical-Bayes solvers at scales where they have not been checked (DECISIONS
   records point-Laplace solver inaccuracy at extreme noise scales). Ranks divided by p give
   per-gene SDs of roughly 0.02 to 0.2, where the 10⁻⁸ floor can zero real programs.
   Per-gene SD scaling puts every gene near SD 1, the scale of the z-scored and X/sd inputs
   on which the floors and solvers have been exercised. (ii) The entries stay positive, so
   the point-exponential factorization stays nonnegative. (iii) Each gene gets equal weight.
   With raw ranks, genes whose ranks spread more would get larger loadings, because one prior
   scale is shared across the genes of a program. (iv) Compared with A's PE arm, only the rank
   transform changes. Compared with the current z-score point-Laplace model, the input and the
   prior change together, which is the comparison asked for. (v) External cohorts follow the
   October 30 rule of scaling by each cohort's own within-cohort SD, which also adjusts for
   their smaller shared gene sets (1,989 to 2,050 genes), since fewer genes shrink the rank
   range.

   *Comparison.* Compare the arm with the current working input,
   z-score within cohort with point-Laplace priors, on the same five-fold training CV:
   fold-wise and mean stratified concordance, the paired difference with its SE, the
   number of live and survival-active programs, convergence, and whether one program
   absorbs the mean level. The arm is descriptive. It is adopted only through a recorded
   DECISIONS.md entry, and it uses no external results. If A already adopts PE, this arm is
   the rank-transform input under the chosen prior and adds no fits. Otherwise it adds five
   fits.
3. **Rank.** K = 7 first came from training CV, but external C later screened out ranks 5
   and 6 and supported keeping 7 (July 13, August 19 and 27). Reselect K by five-fold
   training CV with the one-SE rule, as DeSurv did, over K = 2 to 12 under the priors chosen
   in A and B. Report the number of programs with nonzero loadings and survival-active
   programs at each K, not only the CV curve.

   *Which K this chooses.* With pruning set aside, the fit keeps every column it starts
   with, so the CV chooses the fitted rank, which is K_init. K_eff, the number of programs
   with nonzero loadings, is not chosen. It is what the empirical-Bayes priors leave active
   at the chosen K_init, and it is reported alongside. The priors do this shrinkage
   themselves: a point-mass prior fitted with all its weight at zero sets a program's
   loadings to exactly zero. Two cutoffs remain outside the priors. The fitter's numerical
   dead-program floor zeroes a program whose information falls below 10⁻⁸, and the
   "survival-active" label uses |E[β_k]| ≥ 1.96 posterior SD. The ROADMAP item on
   replacing that label with the posterior inclusion probability (task B) addresses the
   second. DeSurv chose its rank k the same way,
   by CV with the one-SE rule, without a pruning step. If the priors zero out the extra
   columns, the CV curve should flatten above K_eff, and the one-SE rule then picks the
   smallest K_init on the flat part.

## Task G. A results list that rebuilds when the model changes (design only)

**Goal.** Never present a result from an outdated model, and always know which model state
produced each result. Every analysis should come from one consistent set of runner scripts
that call the same fit and predict functions, so that a model update reruns all of the
results that depend on it, not a selection of them. This task writes a design for

**Starting points.** The ROADMAP already has an open item to investigate the R `targets`
package. `targets` records each step (data, fit, table, figure, report) and its inputs,
including the code of the functions it calls. When a function such as `fit_ssbmf_yfb()`
changes, every downstream result is marked out of date and `tar_make()` reruns only those.

rebuild after editing fitter code, but it cannot do the rebuild tracking itself.

## Pruning: set aside (decided by Andrew, 2026-11-09)

After the KL fixes, the final rerun (KL-2, after the flat-β fix) gave the October answer
again on the October 15 bed at starting K = 7. Unpruned YFB recovered the planted program
in 17 and 19 of 20 seeds. Ordinary and protected pruning recovered it in 2 of 20 at each
noise level, with median retained K of 2 and 1. The working fit stays at K = 7 without pruning.

The remedy aimed at the actual failure is `prune_preserve_posterior = TRUE`: the planted
program is lost when the fit restarts after a deletion, and this option keeps the full
posterior instead of restarting. On the pre-fix code it recovered 15 to 19 of 20 but failed
convergence and rank. It has not been run since either fix. A rerun is about 80 fits and
under an hour locally, with its design and criteria written before running. Starting ranks
5, 10 and 15 matter only if that arm passes.

Pruning was meant to start from a large K_init and remove programs that contribute to
neither reconstruction nor survival. That is one way to choose the rank. Task F chooses the
rank the way DeSurv did, by five-fold training CV with the one-SE rule, which needs no
pruning and avoids the restart failure. Pruning is therefore set aside. No
pruning runs are planned, and every fit in tasks A to F is unpruned. The full-posterior
pruning rerun is revisited only if the CV-chosen rank turns out unstable across folds or
seeds. In that case it runs under A's chosen prior, since the prior changes what pruning
sees.
