## Why the priors are reopened

The project set out to fit a nonnegative factorization: point-exponential L and F on
nonnegative expression, as in DeSurv, so that each program is an additive set of genes and
each patient a nonnegative mix of programs. Point-Laplace replaced it on 2026-10-15 for a
narrow reason. On z-standardized input the scores are signed, and the point-exponential
prior collapsed to zero programs in 6 of 10 test-bed seeds. That decision entry says the
real-data refit "will have to settle the L/F prior". It was never settled.

The obstacle is now gone. `center_projection = TRUE` (DECISIONS.md, 2026-11-06) keeps Y
nonnegative for the reconstruction and centers it only inside the Cox projection, which
removes the term that shrank β to zero on uncentered input. A like-for-like comparison of
the two priors is therefore possible, and the remaining tasks inherit whatever prior it
chooses.

## Task A. Priors on L and F

**Question.** On nonnegative input with the Cox projection centered, does the
point-exponential prior on L and F recover a planted prognostic program, with controlled
false activity and convergence, at least as well as the current z-scored point-Laplace
fit? On the training data, does it predict as well under cross-validation?

**Arms.** Both use `canonicalize = TRUE`, `w_S = 1`, Normal β, K = 7, no pruning, at most
300 sweeps and tolerance 10⁻⁵.

1. PL (current): z-score within cohort, `prior_LF = "point_laplace"`.
2. PE: X/sd within cohort (nonnegative), `prior_LF = "point_exponential"`,
   `center_projection = TRUE`.

**Simulation.** The S-9 generator, `yfb_decision_data(seed, p, v, truth_mode = "shared")`
in `results/benchmark_sim/run_yfb_supervision_decision.R`: seeds 1 to 10, p = 2,000,
planted variance shares 0.25%, 1% and 5%, n = 400 training and 120 validation patients,
30 datasets. Its planted L and F are drawn from exponential distributions, so they are
nonnegative and neither prior is favored by the truth. The generator returns X/sd, which
is the PE input, and PL takes its within-cohort z-score, as in S-9. Add the S-14
near-threshold cell (planted share 0.05%, strong survival) from
`results/benchmark_sim/run_yfb_near_threshold.R`, 10 seeds, because that is where
supervision mattered. If saved S-9 and S-14 PL fits reproduce under the exact-time
survival fix (`timefix = FALSE`), reuse them. Otherwise refit PL.

**Real data.** The 273 training patients and the five-fold split used in S-11 and S-15.
Report fold-wise and mean CV concordance with `frozen_reverse_cindex()` and the paired
PE minus PL difference with its SE.

*Design decision for Andrew before running.* X/sd keeps each cohort's mean level, and the
fit is pooled over TCGA and CPTAC. A single projection center leaves the TCGA-CPTAC mean
difference in the Cox projection. Two options:

- (recommended) `strata_id = cohort`, which centers the projection within cohort and gives
  each cohort its own baseline hazard. Stratification was performance-neutral for D4
  (DECISIONS.md, 2026-07-15). The 2026-11-06 entry warns that a program which mainly
  separates cohorts is rescaled by a large factor, so record each program's within-cohort
  score SD.
- No strata and one global center, accepting that a program may absorb platform level.

Whichever is chosen, PL gets the same stratification so that only the prior and the input
differ.

**What is compared.** Per fit: recovery (best |r| between the planted direction and E[F_k]
at least 0.7, the matched program survival-active with correct sign), number of false-active
programs, convergence flag, validation C and calibration slope, as defined in the S-9 plan.
On real data, also the number of live programs, whether one program absorbs the mean
expression level (correlation of its scores with each patient's mean expression), and gene
overlap of the top 100 genes of each PE program with programs 2 and 3 of the current fit.

**Success criteria (PE is adopted if all hold).**

1. Recovery in every simulation cell is at least PL's minus 1 of 10.
2. Mean false-active programs per fit is at most 1 in every cell.
3. At least 9 of 10 fits converge in every cell.
4. Real-data CV concordance is no lower than PL's by more than one SE of the paired
   difference, and every fold converges.

PE is the default this test is checking, because it is the intended model. If it fails
any criterion, PL stays and the failure is recorded with the cell that failed. If PE
passes but uses a program for the mean level, K is not raised in this task. That is
reported, and the rank question goes to the rank-selection work.

**Files.** New runner `results/benchmark_sim/run_lf_prior_comparison.R` (simulation and
training CV in one script, output under `results/benchmark_sim/outputs/lf_prior_2026-11-09/`),
attempt entry in `docs/plans/2026-10-16-round2-attempts-log.md`, findings note, DECISIONS.md,
PROJECT_STATUS.qmd. No change to fitter defaults.

## Task B. β prior

**Question.** Under the L/F prior chosen in A, which β prior should the working model use:
point-Laplace, point-normal or Normal?

**Background.** The 2026-10-15 training CV compared point-Laplace with Normal only (mean C
0.651 and 0.653, paired difference −0.002, SE 0.003) and concluded that "the point-Laplace
β choice stands". The S-11 fit on 2026-11-04 then used Normal and the working model has
used it since, without a decision entry. Point-normal has not been compared under the
current canonical fitter. Before canonicalization, point-normal set every β to zero on
real data and in every CV fold (ROADMAP, "YFB point_normal K-CV collapse"). That may no
longer hold, since β is now on the standardized-score scale.

**Design.** Three arms that differ only in `prior_beta`, all other settings from A's
winner. The same simulation cells and the same five training folds as A. Also record, for
point-normal, each program's posterior inclusion probability, since the ROADMAP item on
replacing the activity cutoff with that probability depends on it.

**What is compared.** Recovery, false-active count, convergence, validation C and
calibration slope in simulation. Paired CV concordance and the number of survival-active
programs on the training data.

**Success criteria.** A sparse prior (point-Laplace or point-normal) replaces Normal if it
meets the recovery, false-activity and convergence criteria of A in every cell, has
validation calibration slope closer to 1 than Normal in at least two of the four cells,
and its CV concordance is no lower than Normal's by more than one SE. If both sparse priors
qualify, choose the one with fewer false-active programs. Otherwise Normal stays. If
point-normal sets every β to zero on the training data, that is reported as a failure, not
fixed inside this task.

**Doc fix needed regardless of the result.** DECISIONS.md says the point-Laplace β choice
stands (2026-10-15 entry), but the working fit uses Normal. Add a dated correction that
names the S-11 runner as the point where Normal was used, says no comparison chose it, and
points to this task. Then record B's result as the decision. The 2026-11-06
projection-centering entry describes the planned nonnegative setup with a point-normal β.
Add a line there pointing to B's decision, so three entries do not name three different
β priors.

**Files.** Same runner as A with a β-prior argument, or a short second runner if that
keeps A's runner simple. DECISIONS.md, findings note.

## Task C. Methylation alone

**Question.** Fitted to methylation alone, does the single-modality model find prognostic
programs that the expression fit does not? Programs with survival signal that expression
does not show would motivate the multimodal model. If methylation adds nothing, the case
for task D rests on interpretation alone.

**Data.** `build_multiomics_cohorts()` in `code/load_multiomics_data.R`: matched TCGA
expression, methylation and survival (up to 150 patients before survival filtering).
Methylation β values are bounded in [0, 1] and nonnegative, so they suit the PE arm
directly after X/sd scaling. Use the existing CpG screen
(`screen_multiomics_features()`) at 10,000 CpGs, computed on training patients only.
ICGC is not used.

**Design.** The chosen model from A and B with Y = methylation. Five-fold CV on the
TCGA patients for concordance. Then one full TCGA fit to describe its programs. Compare
with an expression-only fit on the same matched TCGA patients, so that the comparison is
on identical patients and outcomes.

**What is compared.** CV concordance of methylation alone against expression alone on the
same patients. Program-level overlap: for each methylation program, the largest absolute
correlation between its patient scores and any expression-program score. A methylation
program is "not seen in expression" if that correlation is below 0.5 and it is
survival-active in the full fit and in at least three of five folds.

**Success criteria.** The fit converges in the full fit and in at least four of five
folds. Report the number of methylation-only programs, with no adoption decision. At
n ≈ 150 the CV estimates will be noisy, so the concordance comparison is descriptive.

**Known risk.** Earlier methylation-only fits under the multimodal fitter reached the
sweep cap at 10,000 CpGs with no credible coefficients, and failed at 25,000 (ROADMAP,
2026-10-15). Those used the old fitter. If this fit also fails to converge, stop and
report rather than tuning settings.

**Files.** New runner `results/multimodal_real/run_methylation_single_modality.R`,
aggregate output only (no patient-level files in Git), findings note.

## Task E. LB vs YFB with fold-in prediction

**Question.** With the same input and priors, how do the two survival parameterizations
differ in prediction and in what their programs mean? LB models the hazard through the
latent scores, η = Lβ. YFB models it through the projection of the data on the loadings,
η = (YF)β.

**Interpretation to aim for.** DeSurv factors X ≈ WH with nonnegative gene weights W, and
scores a patient by Z = W′X, a weighted sum of gene levels. In YFB, F plays the role of W
and YF is exactly that projection score. In LB, L is the factor-model score itself: the
posterior of each patient's program activity given all programs at once. With nonnegative
F the two are related by YF ≈ L(F′F) plus noise, so YF mixes the latent scores through the
overlap between programs, and the two coincide only when programs share no genes. For new
patients, YFB needs only Y_new F. LB needs fold-in, which solves for L_new under the stored
priors (`predict_ssbmf_lb_foldin()`). The study should say which score each figure and
coefficient refers to, and what a hazard ratio per unit of each means.

**Design.** Supersedes the ROADMAP's planned LB vs YFB study, with its alignment
requirements kept: the same preprocessed Y (X/sd if A chooses PE), the same priors on L,
F and β from A and B, the same initialization and K = 7. The LB arm separates the survival
weight from the (1 − α) tempering, and the LB dead-program start is controlled by the
same starting values as YFB.

- Simulation: the A cells, and the 2026-11-06 matched comparison settings
  (`results/benchmark_sim/run_lb_yfb_matched_comparison.R`) at w = 1 and 10.
- Training data: five-fold CV on the 273 patients.
- External: one fit per model on all 273 patients, scored on Dijk, Moffitt, PACA-AU array
  and PACA-AU RNA-seq, with PACA-AU donors kept together for intervals. Puleo is not
  scored.

**What is compared.** Recovery, false activity, convergence and validation C in
simulation. Training CV concordance. External concordance per series and the paired
difference. Program content: top genes, overlap with each other and with DeSurv's
programs. The two linear predictors on the same patients: their correlation and the
per-program correlations between L_k and (YF)_k.

**Success criteria.** This task describes the two models and has no adoption rule unless
one model fails A's recovery, false-activity or convergence criteria in a cell where the
other passes. In that case, the failing model is not carried forward. The external
results are reported as descriptions and do not choose the model.

**Files.** Extend `results/benchmark_sim/run_lb_yfb_matched_comparison.R` or a new
`run_lb_yfb_study.R`, findings note, and a short interpretation section suitable for the
progress book.
