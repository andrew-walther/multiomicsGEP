Summary

The K_init consensus sweep was extended from 8 spot values to the full K_init=2..20 grid, and now includes BIC and an ELBO-style joint log-likelihood alongside ELBO and external C-index. A new helper, compute_joint_ll_bic(), combines the genomics reconstruction term already tracked during CAVI with a Cox partial log-likelihood recomputed post-hoc from the returned fit, and derives a BIC (df = K_init·(n+p+1), charged by the CAVI starting K, not by ARD’s K_eff). See §1.

The four criteria do not agree, and that disagreement is reported rather than resolved. ELBO and BIC both prefer K_init=3; external C-index prefers K_init=12, though K_init=7 through 20 mostly sit on a plateau around C≈0.627. K_init=7 is kept as the presented recommendation — survival-active count is stable at 2 from K_init=7 up — but the full curves and a per-K table are shown alongside it rather than only the recommendation, to make the disagreement visible. See §1.

The multi-cohort simulation was rewritten to select K the way the real-data procedure now does: an over-specified K_init pruned by ARD, instead of handing the model the oracle true K. Recovery, specificity, and held-out C-index are flat across K_init=6/12/20 in every scenario — a clean, well-powered confirmation (15 seeds) that over-specifying K and trusting ARD costs nothing relative to knowing the true K. See §2.

A secondary question — does over-specifying K_init increase the false-positive rate on non-signal factors — softened under more seeds, which is itself the finding worth recording. At 5 seeds it looked like a clean improvement; at 15 seeds the improvement in the realistic scenario is directionally consistent but not significant (paired p=0.07), and an apparent improvement in the negative-control scenario turned out to be seed noise (flat at both seed counts once averaged properly). See §2.2.

The 2026-08-27 lab meeting deck is built and dropped the loadings predictor (Lβ) from the narrative entirely. Only the projection predictor (YF)β is presented going forward. See §3.

7.1 §1 — K_init consensus sweep

Extended results/benchmark_sim/run_k_init_sweep.R from 8 spot values (5,6,7,8,9,10,15,20) to the full K_init=2..20 grid, and added a joint log-likelihood / BIC column via a new code/compute_bic.R::compute_joint_ll_bic(). The genomics term is read from the fit’s own history$elbo_proxy at its final iteration (not a naive tail(), which is wrong for any fit that converges early), plus the Gaussian normalizing constant the per-iteration tracker omits. The survival term is recomputed post-hoc from the fit’s returned EF/EBeta — matching exactly what external prediction does — rather than read from a stale in-loop value that predates the post-loop sign-correction step.

ELBO and BIC both prefer the smallest K_init tested (3); BIC’s complexity penalty (~13,100 per unit K_init here) makes this unsurprising once K_init climbs into double digits. External C-index prefers K_init=12, but the plateau from K_init=7 onward is wide — most points in that range sit within a few thousandths of each other. Two isolated dips (K_init=11 and 13) are visible against their neighbors, consistent with the single-seed CAVI factor-collapse artifact already documented for other K values, not a new failure mode.

Decision: K_init=7 is kept as the presented recommendation for the same reason it has been kept before — the survival-active count (2) is stable there and beyond, and it is reachable from a single fresh fit rather than a warm-start procedure. The full sweep is presented alongside it specifically so the disagreement between criteria is visible to the audience, not smoothed over into a single number.

Two open items were raised in review and are tracked in ROADMAP.md, not yet acted on: whether the ARD retention thresholds themselves (PVE>1%, |β̂|>0.001) need a literature grounding and a sensitivity sweep — beta_threshold in particular was reverse-engineered from this model’s own coefficient scale, not drawn from a citable source — and whether a fully Bayesian K-selection alternative (a prior on K itself) is worth scoping as a longer-term replacement for the current post-hoc consensus.

7.2 §2 — Multi-cohort simulation under ARD

results/multi_cohort_sim/run_multicohort_sim.R previously fit every arm at the oracle true K (K=6 in every scenario) and compared 5 arms including the loadings predictor. Rewritten to 2 arms (the projection predictor, and the unsupervised EBMF→Cox baseline) fit at three K_init settings: K_init=6 (the oracle value, retained only as an internal reference point) and K_init=12/20, mirroring the real-data over-specify-then-ARD-prune procedure.

The K_init=6 setting, re-run through the new script, reproduced the original June results almost exactly (e.g. hybrid-scenario held-out C-index 0.815 vs. 0.815) — a useful confirmation that the rewrite changed nothing about the underlying fitting, only which K values get compared.

Across K_init=6/12/20, recovery correlation, specificity-classification accuracy, and held-out C-index are flat in every scenario. Over-specifying K and trusting ARD to prune costs nothing relative to knowing the true K in advance — the core validation this exercise was designed to produce.

7.2.1 §2.2 — A false-positive-rate trend, and a lesson about seed count

A natural follow-up question: does giving ARD more unnecessary factors to work with increase how often it falsely calls a non-signal factor survival-active? At 5 seeds, the answer looked like a clean “no, if anything it improves”: the hybrid scenario’s false-positive rate dropped from 0.45 (K_init=6) to 0.20–0.25 (K_init=12/20), and the negative-control scenario dropped from 0.067 to 0.033.

Re-running at 15 seeds (a --n-seeds override was added to the runner rather than changing the shared seed list in config/globals.yml, which several other simulation scripts also read) showed a less clean-cut pattern. The hybrid-scenario drop is still there directionally (0.35→0.22→0.27) but a paired t-test (same seeds, K_init=6 vs. 12) gives p=0.07 — not conventionally significant. The negative-control scenario’s apparent improvement turned out to be an artifact of only 5 seeds: at 15 seeds it is flat at 0.10 across every K_init tested.

The honest summary is two-layered: the primary metrics (recovery, specificity, C-index) give a well-powered null result with no caveats needed; the false-positive-rate question gives a directionally consistent but not-yet-significant trend, worth watching rather than reporting as settled. Both are presented that way in the 8/27 deck rather than collapsing to a single “ARD helps” headline.

This also lines up with a separate discussion with advisors about the 8/21 finding that ARD can over-count survival-active factors in simulation: a small, non-zero coefficient on a non-signal factor contributes minimally to the risk score in practice, so over-counting in this sense is a lesser concern than it might first appear, independent of whether the false-positive rate itself trends up or down with K_init.

7.3 §3 — The 8/27 lab meeting deck

presentation/walther_lab_meeting_08_27_2026/ is built from the 6/18 deck as a template, not a diff against it — the loadings predictor (Lβ) is dropped from the methods, results, comparison table, and appendix entirely, and the K-selection story is rewritten as the two-stage ARD framework described in §1. The cohort-membership-indicator extension moved out of Methods into Future Directions, reframed as a forward-looking multi-cohort/multi-modality direction rather than a tested-and-rejected result.

The external two-step baseline was reframed to the fairest comparison available (EBMF→Cox, K=40, LASSO stage 2) rather than the smaller/unregularized baseline the June deck used; the headline pooled bootstrap difference (+0.026, 95% CI [0.0002, 0.0498]) was verified to reproduce directly from paired, patient-aligned risk scores before it went on a slide, rather than taken on faith from prior prose.
