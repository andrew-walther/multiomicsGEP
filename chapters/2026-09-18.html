9.1 §1 — Matched multimodal YFB implementation

The new model follows the reviewed derivation in derivations/multimodal_YFB/. Production helpers, preprocessing, updates, fitting, prediction, and simulation live in code/; focused tests live in tests/. The model is intentionally separate from the established single-mode YFB fitter.

For matched expression and methylation blocks,

\[ Y_m=LF_m^\top+E_m, \qquad \eta_i=\sum_k\beta_k\sum_m\sum_jY_{mij}F_{mjk}. \]

\(L\) is a shared nonnegative score matrix, \(F_m\) are modality-specific nonnegative loading matrices, and both modalities retain separate loading priors and feature precisions. Every \(F_m\) update includes its reconstruction term and its Cox-working-model contribution. This is the intended supervised multimodal extension, not an unsupervised factorization followed by Cox regression.

The simulation framework supplies independent matched training and validation cohorts with proportional-hazards survival generated from the same raw joint predictor. It compares joint multimodal YFB with unsupervised EBMF→Cox, expression-only and methylation-only YFB, and a stacked-risk Cox baseline. The full test harness passes (509/509), and all 35 derivation-algebra checks pass. The planned formal multi-replicate K-selection study has not yet been run.

9.2 §2 — Resolving raw-scale convergence

The model has a factor-wise scale symmetry:

\[ (L_{\cdot k},F_{m\cdot k},\beta_k) \mapsto (a_kL_{\cdot k},F_{m\cdot k}/a_k,a_k\beta_k),\qquad a_k>0. \]

This transformation leaves both \(LF_m^\top\) and the raw predictor \(\eta\) unchanged. Initial fits could continue moving along this non-identifiable direction despite stable fitted data and risk scores. The fitter now sets the sample standard deviation of each factor’s raw joint projection to one after its coordinate update, transforming posterior second moments and point-exponential prior rates consistently. This controls representation scale without normalizing the predictor or removing survival feedback.

The stopping rule now tests the relative maximum change in each identifiable fitted reconstruction and in \(\eta\):

\[ \frac{\max|q^{(t)}-q^{(t-1)}|} {\max\{1,\max|q^{(t)}|,\max|q^{(t-1)}|\}} < 10^{-4}. \]

This is invariant to raw modality units. The initial 300-sweep cap was too short for a stable K=7 draw (it required 314 sweeps), so the isolated multimodal cap is 500. Raw latent moment change is retained as a diagnostic but is not a valid convergence criterion under scale exchangeability. A standardized Cox warm start prevents an all-zero coefficient fixed point, and the point-exponential update handles subnormal zero-information floating-point remainders explicitly.

These changes must be recorded in future methods writing: they choose a numerical representative in an equivalent parameterization, rather than changing the fitted likelihood or risk score.

The C-index and held-out partial likelihood peak at \(K_{\mathrm{init}}=12\) in this one draw, but this is not a selected default. Although the fits converge numerically, marginal joint PVE \(\geq1\%\) does not yield a K-invariant effective count, and nominal survival flags increase at some higher ranks. The K-selection runner therefore uses identical simulated training and validation cohorts for every K within a replicate, checkpoints every fit, and will select the smallest K within one standard error of the best mean held-out C-index across independent replicates. Held-out partial likelihood is reported as a secondary, same-risk-set diagnostic. Reconstruction-active, survival-active, and union-retained counts remain separate outputs rather than rank-selection rules.

9.3.1 Adverse and protective program recovery

The new adverse_protective simulation sets the first two true joint programs to \(\beta=(0.70,-0.70,0)\), so successful recovery requires both retention and the correct adverse/protective directions. At \(K_{\mathrm{init}}=12\) on the same held-out draw, the fit converged with six reconstruction-active, four survival-active, and eight union-retained factors; it recovered both true prognostic programs and both signs (prognostic and direction recall = 1.0). The extra nominal survival factors are another reason the final rank must be selected from repeated held-out performance, not coefficient flags alone.

9.3.2 Reconstruction parsimony: tested and deferred extension

The current point-exponential priors shrink individual score and loading coordinates; they are not a valid whole-factor ARD mechanism. A shared Gamma-rate prototype was tested as a factor-level relevance parameter, but it did not reduce the number of reconstruction-active factors and worsened held-out C-index. It was removed rather than retained as a nominal ARD option.

Accordingly, reconstruction parsimony is currently obtained by externally selecting \(K_{\mathrm{init}}\) from paired replicated held-out performance, then reporting the reconstruction/survival retention classes within that fit. A future group spike-and-slab or group-penalty extension would require a new derivation and its own recovery study; it is not part of the current method.

9.4 §4 — Follow-up questions from the September 4 meeting

9.4.1 Coefficient prior

The multimodal derivation uses a Normal (ridge-type) prior for \(\beta\), which has an exact conjugate update. This is consistent with single-mode YFB evidence that a Normal prior is necessary when PDAC survival signal is weak; point-normal shrinkage can collapse coefficients to zero in small folds. Laplace-prior comparison remains a planned sensitivity analysis, not a current model change.

9.4.2 Initialization, K selection, and factor retention

The fitter uses nonnegative-SVD initialization plus a Cox warm start. The current K panel checks K sensitivity, not 100 random starts. Its initial result shows that convergence alone is insufficient: marginal PVE reporting is not a rank-selection method in this over-specified nonnegative factorization. The next evidence must come from paired replicated held-out C-index and partial likelihood, with the fixed-working objective kept as an optimization diagnostic.

9.4.3 Supervision and low-variance prognostic factors

Unlike the single-mode compatibility implementation, survival directly enters the multimodal loading update. A low-variance prognostic-factor scenario is included to test whether this can retain clinical signal that is not dominant in reconstruction. Prognostic activity is currently based on \(|E(\beta_k)|/\operatorname{SD}(\beta_k)\geq1.96\), which is invariant to the factor-scale transformation. Relative-to-largest-\(|\beta|\) thresholding remains a simulation-only sensitivity analysis.

9.4.4 Cohorts and future real data

No matched TCGA/ICGC expression–methylation cohorts are yet in scope, so no cohort indicator or cohort-specific multimodal coefficient has been added. The single-mode results caution that genomics-side cohort indicators can compete with prognostic structure and apparent cohort-specific effects can reflect limited sample size. A future cohort term must specify whether its factor is fixed, whether its survival coefficient is constrained to zero, and how survival comparability is checked.
