3  May 28, 2026

This week answered three open questions from the May 7 entry: which preprocessing actually works for merged multi-platform training, whether a cohort indicator helps, and whether to standardize on LB or YFB. It also tested DeSurv-aligned gene selection as a replacement for plain variance filtering.

3.1 Method updates

  • 18-configuration preprocessing benchmark (5/27). Compared 5 preprocessing strategies (joint quantile normalization + rank transform, per-platform z-standardization, joint QN without rank transform, joint z-standardization, log-only) across LB and YFB, with and without a cohort indicator, on merged TCGA+CPTAC (\(n=273\), 5-fold CV, biological floor \(K\geq3\)).
  • DeSurv-aligned gene selection (5/28). Replaced variance-only gene selection with a combined mean+variance rank computed per-platform before normalization (DeSurv’s own approach), keeping the top 3,000 genes per cohort (2,064 after intersection, vs. 2,000 before).

3.2 Results updates

Critical finding: preprocessing is not a free choice. 10 of the 12 non-per-platform configurations collapsed to \(\hat\beta=0\) for every factor. Only per-platform z-standardization consistently recovers non-zero survival coefficients. Without it, the top singular vectors of the merged matrix capture the RNA-seq/proteomics platform contrast rather than biology, and the survival update has no real biological signal to use.

Cohort indicator helps only in specific configurations, not universally. It absorbs the platform offset when preprocessing hasn’t already removed it (+0.020 for LB with joint QN, meaningfully helpful there), but is neutral to harmful once per-platform z-standardization is already in place (+0.004 for LB, −0.013 for YFB, where at low \(K\) it competes with the 2 biological factors for variance). Recommendation: keep it available as a robustness option for LB when per-platform preprocessing can’t be guaranteed, but exclude it from the primary YFB configuration.

DeSurv gene selection improved external concordance in both model families: +0.012 for YFB (D4 vs. the earlier per-platform-only D2), +0.006 for LB (D3 vs. D1). YFB also outperformed LB on the same DeSurv gene set by +0.014 (D4 vs. D3); the two effects are additive. Every one of the 5 configurations tested (D1–D5) converged to exactly 2 active survival factors regardless of how many factors were requested (K ranged 3–8), with one adverse-direction and one protective-direction program every time. The same 2-factor structure appears in every preprocessing configuration tested, which supports it being a real property of the data rather than a fitting artifact.

3.3 Relevance of new findings

This is the week the recommended configuration actually converges: YFB, DeSurv-aligned gene selection, per-platform z-standardization, no cohort indicator, \(K=7\). It beats the alternatives for reasons we understand: platform-scale collapse avoided, gene selection matches DeSurv, YFB’s exact scoring beats LB’s OLS approximation, not because it happened to score highest in a table of 18/5 configurations.

3.4 Open items to address

  • Pathway enrichment on D4’s 2 active factors (Factor 3 adverse, Factor 7 protective), the main interpretability task left before the biology story can be told.
  • Compare patient loadings on the 2 active factors to Moffitt subtype scores in TCGA to check concordance with classical vs. basal subtypes.
  • YFB’s point-normal prior still returns C=0.5 for all K in cross-validation. Open issue; normal prior remains the default.
  • Cohort indicator may be worth reconsidering once training extends past 2 platforms.
